§ 02  —  Situations

Where an open listing isn't the right fit.

A traditional listing is the right call for plenty of homes — and the wrong one for others. These are the circumstances a direct sale tends to serve better.

i.

Inherited property

Out-of-state heirs, probate delays, siblings with different plans, or a home no one in the family wants to manage or maintain.

ii.

Divorce or separation

A clean, fast exit from a shared asset so both parties can move forward — without the home becoming another thing to argue about.

iii.

Needs significant work

Roof, foundation, water or fire damage, long-deferred maintenance, hoarder situations. Purchased as-is, with no repair obligation on your part.

iv.

Tired landlord

Non-paying tenants, a rental that's become more burden than income, or a property you're simply ready to be done with.

v.

Relocating or downsizing

Moving for work, moving closer to family, retiring out of state, or transitioning to a smaller home — on the schedule you choose.

The Firm

Cardinal & Oak is built on one premise: the traditional process isn't the right fit for every home.

The conventional path — listing with an agent, staging, showings, inspections, negotiations, months of waiting on a lender — works perfectly well for a lot of homes. But it isn't the right fit for all of them.

Some properties are inherited and sitting empty three states away. Some are tied up in a divorce where neither party wants to manage a listing. Some are rentals that have quietly become more trouble than income. Some need work the owner isn't in a position to take on. For those, a direct, private sale is often the cleaner answer.

Cardinal & Oak is a Connecticut home-buying firm built for those situations. When you call, a person answers. When you ask for an offer, we put it in writing within one business day. When you're ready to close, a licensed Connecticut attorney handles the documents — as state law requires for any real-estate closing here.

If a direct sale is the right fit for your property, we'd welcome the call. If it isn't, we'll tell you so, and point you toward a better path where we can.

§ 05  —  Questions

Questions worth asking.

The ones that come up most often on a first call. If yours isn't here, that's a good reason to pick up the phone.

How do you determine your offer?
We look at recent comparable sales in the immediate neighborhood, estimate the work the home will need to reach resale condition, and back into a price that's fair to you and workable for us. The offer letter walks you through that math. The number isn't a mystery.
Are there fees, commissions, or closing costs?
No fees or commissions on our side. We also cover the standard closing costs the seller would normally pay. The dollar figure on our offer is the dollar figure that wires to you at closing.
What condition must the property be in?
Any condition. Outdated kitchens, roof or foundation issues, fire or water damage, hoarder situations, homes that have sat vacant for years. We purchase as-is. Please don't clean out, paint, or repair anything on our account.
How quickly can a closing be scheduled?
As little as seven days if you need speed. If you'd rather have more time — to find a new home, settle an estate, or wait for a lease to turn over — closings can be scheduled out several months. Your calendar, not ours.
Am I obligated by requesting an offer?
Not at all. Read the offer, take it to an attorney, compare it to a broker's valuation, sit on it for a week, or politely decline. You're not obligated until you sign the purchase agreement — and even after that, you have whatever standard contingencies the agreement includes.
Is Cardinal & Oak a real, registered business?
Yes. Cardinal & Oak is a Connecticut-registered home-buying firm. Every closing runs through a licensed Connecticut real-estate attorney, as state law requires for real estate closings here. We're happy to answer any questions about how the firm operates before you share any information about your property.
Do you purchase outside Connecticut?
Our primary market is Connecticut. We occasionally consider nearby properties in neighboring states on a case-by-case basis. If you're uncertain whether your property falls within our scope, a brief call will answer it.
Can I speak with recent sellers for a reference?
We'll be glad to arrange that as our transaction history builds. In the meantime, everything above the signature line on a purchase agreement is in black and white — price, closing date, what we're buying and in what condition. Your attorney will review it before you sign.
