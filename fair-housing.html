Policy

Equal Housing Opportunity.

Our commitment to non-discrimination in Connecticut real-estate transactions.

Cardinal & Oak is an Equal Housing Opportunity firm.

Our commitment

Cardinal & Oak is committed to the letter and the spirit of United States policy for the achievement of equal housing opportunity throughout the nation. We support an affirmative advertising and marketing program in which there are no barriers to obtaining housing because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin.

Federal protections

The Federal Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination in the purchase, sale, rental, or financing of residential real estate on the basis of the following protected classes:

  • Race
  • Color
  • National origin
  • Religion
  • Sex (including gender identity and sexual orientation)
  • Familial status (including families with children under 18)
  • Disability

Connecticut protections

Connecticut law extends fair-housing protections beyond the federal list to also prohibit discrimination on the basis of:

  • Age
  • Marital status
  • Lawful source of income
  • Sexual orientation
  • Gender identity or expression
  • Veteran status
  • Legal hair texture and protective hairstyles (CROWN Act)

How we practice this

Cardinal & Oak operates as a real-estate wholesaler — we contract to purchase properties and assign those contracts to qualified cash buyers. In every step of that process, we commit to the following:

  • Equal evaluation of every property — based only on condition, location, value, and fit with our network's buying criteria. Never on any characteristic of the seller.
  • Consistent terms — every homeowner receives the same disclosures, the same three-business-day cancellation right, and the same attorney-handled closing structure.
  • No steering — we don't direct our buyer network toward or away from any neighborhood based on its demographic composition.
  • Equal advertising reach — our marketing is conducted on an equal basis across Connecticut communities.

If you believe you've been discriminated against

If you believe you've experienced housing discrimination, you may file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development at hud.gov or by phone at 1-800-669-9777. In Connecticut, complaints may also be filed with the Connecticut Commission on Human Rights and Opportunities at portal.ct.gov/chro or by phone at 1-800-477-5737.
